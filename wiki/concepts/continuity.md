# Continuity

A function $f$ is **continuous at $a$** if $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a)$. This single equation packs three requirements: (1) $f(a)$ is defined, (2) the [[limit]] exists, and (3) the two are equal. Geometrically, the graph has no break at $a$ — it can be drawn without lifting the pen.

**Discontinuity types:** removable (a hole), jump (one-sided limits differ), infinite (e.g. $1/x^2$ at 0).

Continuity is closed under arithmetic ($f+g$, $f-g$, $cf$, $fg$, $f/g$ where $g\ne 0$) and under composition. Polynomials, rational, root, and trigonometric functions are continuous on their domains. A major consequence is the [[intermediate-value-theorem]].
