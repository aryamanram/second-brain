# Ecosystem Theory of Harm (EC digital cases)

Findings from **Appendix B** of [[schrepel-digital-brain-for-research]], synthesized from
flagship EC digital-platform decisions (2004–2025).

## Headline finding

The Commission shows **emerging but inconsistently applied ecosystem thinking**. It uses
"ecosystem" as a contextual frame and a qualifying condition for abuse, but **has not
developed a unified ecosystem theory of harm**. Analysis remains predominantly
product-by-product; remedies are narrow (interoperability and access orders) and none
attacks the ecosystem structure itself.

## Where ecosystem reasoning appears explicitly

- **Apple Mobile Payments (AT.40452, 2024)** — the most ecosystem-centric decision and
  the *only* one where ecosystem lock-in drives **market definition** (not just the
  theory of harm). The Commission applies an **after-market analogy**: iOS as a
  vertically integrated ecosystem with consumer lock-in, so dominance in the primary
  market (iOS devices) can confer dominance in adjacent secondary markets (NFC mobile
  wallets) without separate dominance findings there.
- **Google Android (AT.40099, 2018)** — frequent ecosystem/network-effect references,
  but the analysis fragments into separate tying, bundling, and revenue-share counts.
- **Microsoft Teams II (AT.40873, 2025)** — "one-stop-shop" ecosystem framing, but the
  abuse theory remains tying (Teams bundled with Office 365).

## The pattern

In multi-product cases the ecosystem is visible *across* counts but is never the unit of
legal analysis. The Commission asks "does this specific conduct in this specific market
foreclose competitors?" — never "does this firm's ecosystem as a whole foreclose
competition?" Harm theories applied: tying/bundling, market-to-market leveraging,
after-market abuse via lock-in, foreclosure through data advantage, and self-preferencing
— each assessed individually. No decision theorizes a **data flywheel** as a standalone
theory of harm.

## Remedies

Consistently **behavioural** (access, interoperability, non-discrimination) — Apple NFC,
Google Android, Microsoft (2004), Google Adtech. The Commission has **never ordered
structural separation** of a digital ecosystem, implicitly accepting integrated
ecosystems as legitimate so long as specific abusive conduct is corrected.

## The doctrinal path forward

The Apple after-market analogy suggests a route to ecosystem-level analysis: a single
dominance finding generating liability across adjacent markets. The DMA's gatekeeper
framework already operates on this logic, but Article 102 enforcement has not yet adopted
it systematically. The Commission "sits in a transitional position."
