# Institutional Memory Audit

A specific output the [[six-step-research-protocol]] produces when applied to an
**institution's own decisional corpus** (e.g. competition decisions, judicial opinions,
regulatory guidance), as described in [[schrepel-digital-brain-for-research]]. It differs
from applying the protocol to a *scholarly* corpus.

## What it reveals

- **[[god-nodes]] = self-declared foundations.** The most-connected decisions reveal
  which of its own rulings the institution's case law has treated as foundational — not
  by editorial judgment, but by the pattern of citations and conceptual dependencies the
  institution itself created over time.
- **Disconnected components = doctrinal orphans.** Decisions adopted but never followed
  up, theories of harm articulated but never developed, markets investigated once and
  never revisited.

## What it tells the institution

What its accumulated decisions have established as central, where its enforcement record
has gaps, and which lines of reasoning were started and left incomplete. For an antitrust
agency, the audit flags which decisions a case handler should read before drafting, which
market definitions have never been tested on appeal, and which theories of harm appear in
the record without a supporting line of decisions behind them.

This is the institutional-scale counterpart to the [[absence-first-research]] orientation,
and it is the lens behind the EC findings in [[computational-antitrust]] and
[[ecosystem-theory-of-harm]].
