# The Lazy Obsidian Method

A low-maintenance recipe for turning messy notes into a useful AI second brain,
described by [[bryce-robbie]] in [[lazy-obsidian-method-brief]]. It is an *assembly* of
existing tools around [[andrej-karpathy]]'s [[llm-knowledge-base]] method rather than a
new methodology.

## The guiding principle

> "Capture messy. Process later. Let the vault improve over time."

The "lazy" framing is the point: the system should be **easy to use and easy to
maintain**, improving in the background rather than demanding constant curation. Karpathy's
LLM-wiki idea sits at the centre (capture raw → process → link → compound); the value is
in the **support layer** around it — structure, search, graph, and recurring jobs.

## The stack

Six layers (full table in [[lazy-obsidian-method-brief]]):

- **Vault** — [[obsidian]] (local-first markdown).
- **Method** — the Karpathy [[llm-knowledge-base]] / [[digital-brain]] approach.
- **Structure** — [[para-method]] (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive).
- **Workflow** — [[obsidian-skills]] (by [[kepano]]).
- **Graph** — [[graphify]].
- **Search** — [[qmd]].

The "lazy unlock" is the automated [[maintenance-cadence]] (daily ingest / nightly review
/ weekly vault) that keeps the system alive without making maintenance a job.

## Relationship to this wiki and its sources

The method overlaps heavily with the [[llm-knowledge-base-toolchain]] already documented
here — and notably names three tools this wiki uses ([[obsidian]], [[graphify]],
[[qmd]]). Its distinct contributions relative to [[karpathy-llm-knowledge-bases]] and
[[schrepel-digital-brain-for-research]] are (1) an explicit folder taxonomy
([[para-method]]) and (2) a formal recurring-jobs cadence ([[maintenance-cadence]]).
