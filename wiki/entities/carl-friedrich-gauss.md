# Carl Friedrich Gauss

**Carl Friedrich Gauss** (1777–1855) was a German mathematician and physicist, often called the "Prince of Mathematicians" and ranked among the greatest in history. His contributions span number theory (*Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*), the **normal (Gaussian) distribution**, non-Euclidean geometry, differential geometry, astronomy, geodesy, and electromagnetism.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart's Calculus]] he is associated with the **Divergence Theorem** (§16.9), also called the **Gauss–Ostrogradsky theorem**, which relates the outward flux of a vector field through a closed surface $S$ to the divergence integrated over the solid region $E$ it encloses:
$$\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=\iiint_E \operatorname{div}\mathbf{F}\,dV.$$
The result was also obtained independently by the Russian mathematician **Mikhail Ostrogradsky** (1801–1862). It is the third of the three fundamental theorems of vector calculus, alongside [[george-green|Green's]] and [[george-stokes|Stokes']] theorems, and underlies **Gauss's law** in electromagnetism.
