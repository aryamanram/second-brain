# George Green

**George Green** (1793–1841) was an English mathematician and physicist, largely **self-taught** — a miller's son from Nottingham who ran his father's windmill and studied mathematics on his own, with almost no formal education. He published his major work, *An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism*, privately in 1828; it went largely unnoticed until rediscovered and championed by William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) after Green's death. He finally entered Cambridge as an undergraduate at age 40.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart's Calculus]] he is the namesake of **Green's Theorem** (§16.4), which relates a line integral around a simple closed plane curve $C$ to a double integral over the region $D$ it bounds:
$$\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)\,dA.$$
Green's Theorem is the two-dimensional special case of both **Stokes' Theorem** and the **Divergence Theorem** — all generalizations of the [[fundamental-theorem-of-calculus|Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]]. His broader legacy includes **Green's functions** and **Green's identities**, foundational tools in physics and the theory of partial differential equations.
