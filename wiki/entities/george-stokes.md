# George Gabriel Stokes

**Sir George Gabriel Stokes** (1819–1903) was an Irish-born British mathematician and physicist, Lucasian Professor of Mathematics at Cambridge (the chair once held by [[isaac-newton|Newton]]). He made wide-ranging contributions to fluid dynamics (the **Navier–Stokes equations**), optics, and the theory of light, and gave his name to the **stokes** unit of kinematic viscosity.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart's Calculus]] he is the namesake of **Stokes' Theorem** (§16.8), which generalizes Green's Theorem to surfaces in space — relating the surface integral of the curl of a field over an oriented surface $S$ to the line integral around its boundary curve $C=\partial S$:
$$\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_S \operatorname{curl}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}.$$
The theorem actually first appeared as a problem Stokes set on a Cambridge examination; its earliest known statement was in a letter to him from Lord Kelvin. It is one of the three fundamental theorems of vector calculus, alongside [[george-green|Green's]] and the Divergence ([[carl-friedrich-gauss|Gauss]]) theorems.
