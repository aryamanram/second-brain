# Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)

German mathematician and astronomer. Working from the vast body of precise astronomical observations compiled by Tycho Brahe, Kepler published (beginning 1609) three empirical laws describing the motion of the planets — overturning the assumption of circular orbits.

**Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion:**
1. A planet revolves around the sun in an **elliptical** orbit with the sun at one focus.
2. The line joining the sun to a planet sweeps out **equal areas in equal times** (the radius vector's areal velocity is constant).
3. The square of the **period** of revolution is proportional to the **cube** of the length of the semi-major axis of the orbit.

Kepler stated the laws as empirical descriptions; [[isaac-newton|Isaac Newton]] later **derived** all three mathematically from his law of universal gravitation, a landmark demonstration of calculus applied to physics.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart, Calculus (9th Edition)]], Kepler's Laws appear in [[ch10-parametric-and-polar|§10.6]] (Conic Sections in Polar Coordinates), where the polar equation $r=\frac{ed}{1+e\cos\theta}$ of an ellipse models a planetary orbit with the sun at the focus (the pole).
