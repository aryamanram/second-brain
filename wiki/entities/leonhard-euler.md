# Leonhard Euler (1707–1783)

Swiss mathematician, the leading mathematician of the mid-18th century and one of the most prolific in history. Born in Basel, Switzerland; spent most of his career at the academies of science in St. Petersburg and Berlin. Lost the use of one eye in his early 30s and was nearly totally blind for the last 17 years of his life; nonetheless, with a prodigious memory and his powers of mental calculation, he continued to produce mathematics — dictating his discoveries to helpers. His collected works fill ~100 volumes.

Euler is credited with making the notation $e\approx 2.71828$ standard (the base of the [[ch06-inverse-functions|natural exponential function]]) and is honored with two of mathematics' most famous numbers being named for him.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart, Calculus (9th Edition)]], Euler appears in a biographical note attached to **Euler's method** ([[ch09-differential-equations|§9.2]]) — the simplest numerical scheme for approximating solutions of differential equations by stepping along the direction field ($y_{n+1}=y_n+h\,F(x_n,y_n)$).
