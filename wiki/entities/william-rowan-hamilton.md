# William Rowan Hamilton (1805–1865)

Irish mathematician, a precursor of the inventors of vector analysis. Latin, Greek, and Hebrew were said to be among the languages he could read at age five; later he made fundamental contributions to optics, dynamics (the Hamiltonian formulation of mechanics), and algebra. In 1843 he discovered **quaternions** — a four-dimensional number system — from which the modern notions of the **dot product** and **cross product** of vectors were later distilled. At age 22, while still an undergraduate at Trinity College Dublin, he was appointed Professor of Astronomy at the university and Royal Astronomer of Ireland.

In [[stewart-calculus-9e/_hub|Stewart, Calculus (9th Edition)]], Hamilton appears in a biographical note attached to the **cross product** ([[ch12-vectors-geometry-of-space|§12.4]]), reflecting that the cross product descends from his quaternion algebra.
